01 · Engineering · 2025–26

Low-Cost Autonomous Wheelchair Attachment

A wheelchair that stops on its own.

Many people who use a manual wheelchair lack the strength to push it, and powered chairs are expensive and hard to get. So I built a removable, bolt-on attachment: a Segway scooter drives the chair, a Raspberry Pi 4 reads an ultrasonic sensor, and a servo on a mount I 3D-printed presses and releases the throttle. Nothing is rewired, and it comes off in under 30 minutes.

My biggest lesson: stopping isn’t the same as stopping comfortably. When testers found hard stops uncomfortable, I wrote a gradual-deceleration routine, and rider comfort rose from 2.6 to 4.0 out of 5.

It also won the IEEE Award and ASME 1st Place.

02 · Business plan & pitch · 2026

Self-Drive and Thrive

A wheelchair you steer with a wave.

My business plan and pitch for a $350 gesture-controlled electric wheelchair, about one-tenth the price of a traditional one. It’s built on affordable microcontrollers, DC motors, lithium-ion batteries and hand-gesture sensors. Wave right to turn right, move forward to accelerate, show a stop gesture to brake.

It builds on the “future work” list from my wheelchair attachment, and it maps the market, competitors, costs, marketing and risks.

03 · Code · 2024–25

WeatherWear

Weather in, outfits out.

Raw forecast numbers don’t tell you what to wear, so I built a Python app with a Kivy interface that does. Enter a zip code and it pulls live weather from OpenWeatherMap and WeatherAPI, maps ten conditions onto researched ranges, and suggests clothing, hairstyles, makeup and skincare.

It grew from a temperature-only terminal prototype, and four months of testing with friends shaped the rest. The goal: “lower your decisions without lowering your comfort.”
